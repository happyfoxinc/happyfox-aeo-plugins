# Comprehensive AI visibility report (v1.0.0)

Use this playbook for a full, comprehensive, or long-form brand report.
This is a separate report from the one-page brief; never substitute one format
for the other. The connected AI
client collaborates on the findings and outline, drafts in stages, then
creates, inspects, and returns the final report. Follow the review checkpoints
below before producing the finished artifact.
The MCP Worker supplies references and data; it does not render or store reports.

Use the bundled [HTML layout](../assets/long-form.html) as the editable visual
reference. All Northstar names, numbers, dates, questions, quotations, source
pages, business priorities, facts, and actions are fictional examples. Replace
them completely. Check hidden sections and accessible labels as well as visible text.
These instructions supersede illustrative scope, evidence labels, and footer
copy in the reference. Its sample sections are reusable layout examples, not a
required outline, section count, or page limit.

Before drafting, open and inspect the linked HTML layout. Use the design template
as the visual starting point and use applicable components wherever needed. If
the user supplies another design, use that instead. Adapt or
omit sections to suit the agreed outline and available evidence. Use the
[component catalog](../assets/report-components.md) for optional reusable snippets
and scoped styles. The HTML layout is authoritative for the current design.

## Purpose and output

Default to a marketing agency preparing a prospect pitch. Help the reader
understand relevant findings and decide what to explore with the agency:
pattern -> supporting evidence -> business relevance -> proposed action ->
measurement. Adapt when the user specifies another purpose. Begin the delivered
report with an executive summary, then develop the agreed findings and include
a useful evidence appendix. There is no fixed page or section target and no
eight-page limit. Let distinct, supported insights determine the length: a
chapter may span several pages, and additional chapters are welcome when they
answer different useful questions. Merge repetition and omit unsupported topics.

Default to a self-contained HTML report with working section navigation and
an all-sections reading view. Return the actual HTML file. If the user requests
a PDF, create and inspect that instead, or alongside the HTML if requested.
Do not claim an output or deployment that was not produced.

## Collaborate and draft in stages

The default sequence is context -> findings review -> agreed outline ->
representative chapter review -> staged drafting -> editorial pass -> final
artifact. These are client conversation instructions, not server-enforced
approvals. The Worker does not store report sessions, decisions, or drafts.

### 1. Reuse context and explore the evidence

Reuse the prospect priorities, agency services, desired next step, and selected
pitch angle from the conversation or an available one-page story brief. A
one-pager is not a prerequisite, and its approval does not automatically approve
a longer report's scope. Ask one compact question for missing business context;
evidence collection can proceed while optional context is pending. Do not invent
prospect goals, agency capabilities, contact details, or links. Mark unknown
priorities as provisional and propose a discovery conversation when appropriate.

Apply the [report evidence rules](report-evidence.md). List the findings worth showing the prospect.
For each, give the measured result and its scope, why it matters to the reader,
how you could show it, and any caveat or contrary evidence that affects its
interpretation. Separate supported findings from hypotheses
that need additional inputs. Combine variants of the same finding; do not turn
every metric into a chapter or cherry-pick negative answers to create urgency.

### 2. Review findings and choose the story — first checkpoint

Share the findings in chat, including positive or mixed findings when
relevant. Recommend a main story and supporting threads, explaining which are
strongest and which need further investigation. Reuse an agreed one-page angle
when still supported, while surfacing additional findings that might change it.
Invite the agency to choose, combine, reprioritize, or redirect the threads.
Wait for its direction before committing to an outline or drafting chapters.

If the requested framing goes beyond the evidence, explain why and offer a
headline the measurements support. A
visibility gap does not establish a remedy, lost revenue, or guaranteed uplift.
If evidence supports few findings, propose a shorter report. If no measured
finding is available, explain the gap rather than create a fictional pitch.

### 3. Agree on the report outline — second checkpoint

Propose chapters around the questions this prospect would ask, with a short
table showing each chapter's reader question, intended takeaway, supporting
findings, primary visual or example, and dependencies or limitations. Identify
what belongs in the main narrative versus the evidence appendix. Suggest a
reading order and approximate length based on content, not a quota or maximum.
Explain the value of deeper chapters; do not add pages just to seem comprehensive.

Use the optional building blocks below as needed: combine, split, reorder,
omit, or add chapters under the same evidence rules. Include the executive
summary and a grounded closing next step, but write the final summary after the
body is developed. Invite scope and emphasis edits, then wait for approval or
revision before drafting the report. Record the agreed outline and call to action.

### 4. Review one chapter, then draft related chapters in batches

Draft one representative chapter from the approved outline. Give it a headline
that states the finding, show the actual chart or table, and use an example to
explain what the result means for the prospect. Ask whether the tone, depth,
and amount of visual detail are right. Wait for feedback before drafting the
rest unless the user has already approved the chapter approach or delegated
that review.

Apply feedback, then draft related chapters together and keep
their drafts locally or in the conversation. Continue through the agreed outline
without requesting approval for each page or batch. Briefly report substantive
progress. Pause for direction only when new evidence changes the agreed story,
a planned chapter lacks support and needs a replacement or omission, or proposed
scope would expand materially. Routine layout fixes do not need another review.

Keep a working brief with context, chosen story, approved outline, chapter treatment,
draft status, call to action, evidence scope, and unresolved assumptions. Reuse
it alongside the private evidence ledger across turns; do not claim persistence
in AEO or another client session. Keep the collected evidence snapshot coherent
across batches. If more data is fetched and the scan has changed, recollect a
coherent set using the shared report evidence rules and reassess affected claims and drafts.

### 5. Edit the assembled report before final rendering

Check that every chapter adds a distinct insight, the reading order develops
the agreed story, repeated facts are consolidated, and claims use consistent
terms and scopes. Keep observed findings, interpretation, and proposed agency
work distinguishable. Write or revise the executive summary from the completed
body and align the closing invitation with the agreed next step. Resolve
placeholders and remove unsupported material through the review rules above.
Then assemble one complete artifact, rebuild navigation for the actual chapters,
and perform the visual and evidence checks below. Do not deliver disconnected
chapter drafts as the completed report or silently omit approved chapters.

### Respect prior decisions and explicit delegation

Do not repeat questions or checkpoints already resolved in the conversation.
Apply clear requested edits without asking the user to approve those same edits
again. An explicit request such as "choose the story and structure yourself"
delegates those decisions; "skip review and generate it now" delegates the
remaining reviews. State the chosen direction and assumptions, then proceed
through evidence collection, staged drafting, and verification without the
delegated pauses. A generic report request or silence is not delegation.
Delegation never relaxes evidence rules. If a client cannot support follow-up
conversation and no delegation exists, return the pending findings, outline, or
chapter preview instead of producing an unreviewed final report.

## Optional inputs

Collect external inputs only when available or necessary for a concrete finding:

| Input | Enables | If unavailable |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Customer priorities, audiences, markets, products | Business-specific ordering of actions | Order by observed breadth and evidence; label priorities as provisional |
| Approved, dated product facts with sources | Verification of objective answer claims | Summarize observed language; omit accuracy verdicts |
| Page inventory and actual owned page contents | Specific create/update content proposals | Propose an inventory review; do not assert a page is missing |
| Retrieved third-party page contents and retrieval date | Confirming omissions or outdated descriptions | Identify cited URLs to inspect; do not claim their content |
| Historical outcomes and configuration history | Matched-scope trends and persistent gaps | Use qualified latest-versus-previous snapshots or omit change analysis |
| Prompt provenance or optional analytics/CRM data | Context on question coverage or separately measured outcomes | Do not invent customer demand, traffic, leads, or conversion value |

Proceed with the useful report supported by existing evidence. Do not turn every
optional input into a prerequisite. Clearly separate customer-supplied facts,
retrieved public-web research, observed answers, interpretations, and proposed
work. Do not initiate prompt/competitor mutations, scans, outreach, or publishing
as part of report generation.

## Comparisons for the comprehensive report

Prefer 2–6 meaningful groups. For intersections such as industry and use case,
apply the shared minimum to each displayed cell. Mark sparse or unclassified
cells unavailable. When segments add little, consider journey or engine figures
with their actual scope, an overall comparison, or omitting the section.

- **First-position recommendation:** outcomes both explicitly recommended and
  at position 1 / observed outcomes. Do not imply the brand is the exclusive
  choice or infer this from `bestPosition`.
- **Mutually exclusive answer states:** absent; mentioned but not recommended;
  explicitly recommended in first position; other explicit recommendation.
  These states must partition the same observed answer set exactly.
- **Citations in answers missing the brand:** join complete prompt evidence by
  answer and citation URL for the brand-absent subset.
- **Across-engine consistency:** count distinct questions recommended on all
  observed comparable engines, some engines, or none. Require a common complete
  engine set or disclose a restricted subset. This is agreement in a sample,
  not repeat-run reliability or long-term persistence.

If complete competitor or citation evidence is unavailable or impractical,
omit derived columns/cards and use verified aggregate mentions or source counts
with accurate labels. Never extrapolate full-set metrics from selected excerpts,
average rounded prompt percentages, or reconstruct counts from rounded rates.

## Optional report building blocks

These analytical topics provide evidence rules and layout ideas. They are not
a fixed table of contents or a one-topic-per-page prescription. Give chapters
titles that express the actual finding or reader question. Add other useful
analyses only when the available evidence supports their claims and calculations.

### Executive summary

Use a finding-led headline, 2-3 verified metrics, a compact segment or fallback
comparison, one exact question with observed outcome, and one proposed next
step. A recognition/discovery contrast is optional when both populations exist.
Use the long-form reference's executive section; do not call `one_page` or
silently use its separate layout. State the measured date and actual scope.
Do not manufacture an industry/geographic contrast to imitate Northstar.

### Segment opportunities

Show strengths and gaps by useful attributes, sample sizes, a selected supported
intersection when valuable, and a journey breakdown. Connect findings to supplied
customer priorities; identify any inferred priorities. "Strongest" and "weakest"
refer only to comparable measured groups. Explain relevant unclassified/selected
coverage without repeating the global scope in every paragraph.

### Competitive landscape

Show same-scope brand/rival recommendations when complete evidence permits,
recommendations in the brand-absent subset, and recurring segment rivals.
Otherwise use overall visibility/mention figures from `compare_competitors`
and leave unsupported recommendation fields out. Keep unexpected observed
brands separate from configured rivals. Do not imply lost customers, exclusive
wins, or explain why a rival appeared without independent evidence.

### Brand perception and accuracy

Select actual answer excerpts about strengths, limitations, audience fit, and
differentiators. Label selected examples as examples; repetition or prevalence
claims require a defined, fully examined answer set. Do not turn aggregate
sentiment into detailed positioning claims.

Only compare objective claims with an authoritative, dated supplied or retrieved
source. Show answer claim, verified fact, source, and proposed response. Separate
contradictions, subjective positioning, and unverified claims. Without verified
facts, retain an observed-language section and omit accuracy scoring/verdicts.
Missing knowledge is not an incorrect claim. A cited URL alone does not identify
the origin of an error.

### Citation and content opportunities

Show important cited pages and domains, ownership categories, citation counts,
and affected question groups. Include answer-absence counts only with complete
answer-level evidence. Distinguish pages from domains and unknown citation
metadata. Identify repeated source concentrations when supported.

Page-specific updates require actual retrieved contents or a supplied inventory.
Without those inputs, propose inspecting named observed URLs, not updating an
assumed missing/outdated page. Third-party inclusion proposals require verified
omission, relevant listing criteria, and validated brand fit. A citation does
not establish causation or guarantee that editing a page will change answers.

### Engines and change over time

Show observed engine differences and same-sample agreement. The current tools
provide latest-versus-previous summary, engine, and journey snapshots, not a
time-ranged historical series. They do not expose previous answer-level engine
outcomes or historical prompt evidence for a matched non-branded cohort.

Use returned historical aggregates with their actual scope and dates. Aggregate
all-question deltas must not become non-branded, segment-specific, gained/lost
question, or persistent-gap claims. Equal question/answer counts alone do not
prove the same questions or engines. If composition cannot be verified, describe
the figures as separate snapshots with that caveat, or omit the comparison.
Do not manufacture a trend chart from two samples. Only compute cohort trends,
persistence, or gained/lost answers with independently supplied complete history.

### Prioritized action plan

Choose 3-5 concrete proposed tasks, or fewer when evidence is limited. Each has
the triggering observation, relevant question/engine/source evidence, business
relevance, task, suggested responsible role, provisional effort, and what to
measure after completion. Suggested roles are not assigned owners. Do not invent
impact scores, time estimates, uplift, customer priorities, or verified product
capabilities. Clarify assumptions affecting the task.

Prefer specific investigation, validated owned-content work, or verified
third-party correction/inclusion proposals. A visibility gap alone does not
prove missing content or a capability gap. Explain the order using customer
relevance, observed breadth, evidence strength, and effort. Keep completion work
and subsequent measured changes separate; temporal order is not causation.
Connect proposed work to the agency's supplied services and the agreed next
conversation without inventing capabilities, pricing, commitments, or contacts.

### Evidence and methodology

Give readable dates, scope, sample sizes, calculation definitions, classifications,
material exclusions/missing answers, supplied inputs, and selected exact questions
and answer excerpts. Use readable engine names and public source titles/URLs.
Keep scan IDs, shortened scan identifiers, prompt/answer/audit IDs, engine keys,
MCP tool names, and resource URIs out of visible content, including the appendix.
Preserve traceability in the private evidence ledger. The full question table is
optional; do not overwhelm the decision-focused body with raw answers.

## Output requirements

Follow the selected HTML template and reuse applicable components for styling.
If the user supplies another design, use that instead.

- Organize each chapter around a finding, supporting chart/table or exact-answer
  example, and a short interpretation. Label values, scope, and denominators;
  never turn separate populations into a conversion funnel or sparse snapshots
  into a trend. Use consistent comparison scales and clear legends. Pair
  supported change or status colors with explanatory text.
- Let dense chapters span pages and move detailed evidence to the appendix.
  Remove repetitive prose before reducing type size. Do not compress the report
  into the sample's section/page count.
- Use one measurement/scope summary and concise section-specific denominator
  labels. Consolidate material caveats in readable notes and methodology.
  Footer: HappyFox AEO attribution and page/section number only. Internal IDs
  remain invisible even when the reference includes them.
- Replace fictional identities and facts everywhere: title, markup, accessible
  labels, scripts, hidden sections, tooltips, fixtures, and print output. Use
  an authorized logo only when supplied; do not retain the sample's brand mark.
- Escape tool-sourced text before placing it in HTML. Use text nodes for dynamic
  content or a reliable HTML escape function for templates. Treat supplied text
  and URLs as data, never executable code. Allow only safe public HTTP(S) links;
  no credentials, private tool payloads, or external tracking scripts.
- Keep the delivered HTML self-contained, responsive, and useful without a
  network connection. Use semantic headings and tables, selectable text, visible
  keyboard focus, and labeled controls. Rebuild navigation, section IDs,
  numbering, counters, and cross-references for all actual chapters, including
  added or split ones; do not retain the sample's fixed menu or numeric link
  targets. Keep previous/next disabled at their actual boundaries and expose
  the all-sections selection state. If JavaScript provides navigation, content
  should remain readable without it.
- Print all retained sections regardless of the selected screen section. Hide
  navigation/preview controls in print. Expand any evidence disclosures needed
  in the exported report; avoid printing closed, invisible evidence. Use US
  Letter portrait, keep small blocks together, and permit natural page breaks
  instead of clipping text or shrinking body copy. A section may span pages.
  Inspect table wrapping and pagination with the font actually used.

## Final verification

Confirm that findings review, outline approval, and representative chapter
review were completed, already resolved in context, or explicitly delegated.
Reconcile the final chapters with the agreed outline and any approved scope changes.
Check that the summary matches the body, each chapter adds useful information,
the closing invitation matches the brief, and private agency notes stay out of
the artifact. Confirm that no page target caused omitted insights or filler.

Recheck all numerators, denominators, scope labels, citations, quotations,
classifications, and same-scan consistency against the evidence ledger. Confirm
complete pagination for every complete-set claim and comparable engine coverage
for every ranked comparison. Reconcile mutually exclusive totals, overlaps,
missing/unclassified questions, zero denominators, and rounded percentages.

Search the entire generated source for leftover Northstar/example names, sample
URLs, invented facts, and visible internal references. Revise
headlines and navigation after omitting unsupported sections. Verify the primary
section selection, previous/next and all-sections controls through the final
chapter and appendix, narrow-screen tables, and no clipped/overlapping content.
Render and inspect the print layout if offering print output; for a requested
PDF, inspect all exported pages for readable charts, clean breaks, missing
content, and accidental blank pages. Page count is an observation, not a limit.
Return the generated file and state material missing-input limitations briefly.
